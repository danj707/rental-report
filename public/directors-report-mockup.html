Q2 in one paragraph: Watertown Recreation collected $374,769 net this quarter, up 7.8% over Q1 on 58% more transactions — growth came from volume, not price. Summer registration drove 1,771 enrollments across 78 programs, and outdoor season took facility rentals from a winter trickle (219) to 3,211 reservations, with Arsenal Park alone generating $22.3K. 1,006 new households joined the platform — a third of the entire community base arrived this quarter. Demand is now visibly outrunning supply in adult racquet sports: Adult Tennis Beginner carries a waitlist 2.3× its capacity.
Opportunity
Adult tennis & pickleball demand is 2×+ capacity
Adult Tennis Beginner has 53 people waiting on 28 seats across two sections (229% and 150% pressure). Offers sent this quarter were claimed in a median of 6.7 hours — the demand is real and fast. One added section per level would convert an estimated 40+ waitlisted adults at ~$120/seat.
Signal
Facility rentals just became a real revenue line
Rental revenue grew from $527 in Q1 to $38,973 in Q2 — Arsenal Park Facility Rentals is now the #3 GL account. 75% of reservations come from verified residents; 20% booked instantly with no staff touch.
Opportunity
Self-service absorbed the volume spike
Transactions rose 58% QoQ, but staff-entered orders rose only marginally — online self-service took 75% of orders ($180.9K). The counter didn't feel the summer rush; that's capacity you can reinvest in programming.
Risk
Special events are launching far under capacity
Five programs filled below 10% — the two Community Paddle events sold 10 of 400 seats, and Popcorn & Pajamas sold 10 of 150. Capacity settings may be aspirational, but if not, these need marketing or consolidation before they run.
