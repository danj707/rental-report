The GreenPlay cost recovery pyramid

The benchmark parks and recreation departments are measured against, and the reason the tiers exist. Its base is community benefit — open play, a summer concert, programming a town funds because everyone gains from it, which is meant to recover a quarter to a half of what it costs. Its apex is individual benefit — a trip, a private lesson, a specialty class one household gains from, which is meant to recover all of it and then some. A tier is not a grade: recovering 35% on a tier 1 program is the model working. Tiers are yours to set — a program with no tier is counted in the money above and left out of this panel.

Overhead and other lines

Anything not tied to one program — a trip, a special event, a grant, insurance, a vehicle. Built the way you already budget: an account with its line items underneath, each tagged to a category like staff wages, supplies or maintenance, and rolled up above so it reads at a glance instead of scrolling. Picking a category from the list sets whether the line is money in or out, so an expense cannot land in the income column by accident — your own category names still work, they just leave that to you. Give a row a season or a date range and it lands in the periods it belongs to, exactly as a program does; a row with neither is counted below and left out rather than dropped into whichever period happens to be open. These stay apart from program costs on purpose: the tier bands on the P&L tab are direct cost recovery bands, so folding the truck into a swim lesson would push it under its tier for a reason that has nothing to do with the swim lesson. The × at the end of a row deletes that line.
